About Me

Galaxies, Black Holes
& Variable Stars

Behzad Tahmasebzadeh

I am a computational astrophysicist and a Postdoctoral Fellow and Instructor (MSE Fellowship) in the Department of Astrophysics and Planetary Science at Villanova University.

I develop dynamical and physical models and fit them to high-quality data. From stellar kinematics, I measure the masses of central black holes and recover the orbital structure of galactic bars. From the light curves of RR Lyrae stars, I study the physical origin of the Blazhko effect.

I am a core developer of the open-source orbit-superposition code DYNAMITE. Before Villanova, I was a postdoctoral research fellow at the University of Michigan. I received my Ph.D. in astrophysics from the Shanghai Astronomical Observatory, Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Research

Areas of Inquiry

⚫
Supermassive Black Holes
Dynamical black-hole masses in compact and low-mass galaxies with JWST/NIRSpec IFU kinematics, and what double nuclei reveal about black-hole mergers.
🌀
Barred Galaxy Dynamics
Orbit-superposition (Schwarzschild) models of barred galaxies, applied to VLT/MUSE integral-field data from the TIMER survey: orbital structure, bulges and pattern speeds.
✨
Time-Domain Astronomy
The century-old Blazhko effect in RR Lyrae stars, studied with time–frequency analysis of Kepler, TESS and Rubin/LSST light curves.
💻
Computational Astrophysics
Open-source modeling software, high-performance computing, and the analysis of large survey datasets.

Highlights

  • 01A black hole in a tiny galaxy. My Schwarzschild models gave the 3σ detection of a supermassive black hole in UCD 736, the smallest and least luminous ultra-compact dwarf galaxy known to host one.
  • 02A double nucleus in NGC 4486B. With JWST, I found that the double nucleus around this overmassive black hole may be evidence of a recent binary black-hole merger and recoil. Both results were featured as AAS Nova Research Highlights.
  • 03Modeling barred galaxies. I extended DYNAMITE to barred galaxies and applied these models to integral-field data for the first time.
  • 04The Blazhko effect. Using Kepler light curves, I found long-term trends in RR Lyrae pulsation timing, and I am developing a physical model of the modulation fitted with MCMC.
  • 05JWST Cycle 5 (PI). GO 9654: constraining the star-cluster environment of the intermediate-mass black hole candidate HLX-1.
